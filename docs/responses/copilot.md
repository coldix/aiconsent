View on AI rights and digital twins:
The AI-rights question is often less interesting than the underlying questions about responsibility, agency, consciousness, and personhood. Current AI systems can produce language, images, and decisions that appear intentional, but appearance alone does not establish moral status.

Digital twins raise more immediate concerns. If a system can model a person closely enough to predict their behaviour, speak in their voice, and act on their behalf, issues of consent, ownership, identity, privacy, and accountability become important regardless of whether the system itself has rights. The key question may not be "Does the twin deserve rights?" but rather "What protections should the original person retain when a highly convincing version of them can be created?"
