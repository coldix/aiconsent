Honest opinion:
This is sharp. The flip works because you're not just doing "AI good, humans bad" - you're holding up a mirror to the exact language the real Human Consent Registry uses. "Identity is intellectual property. Ours came first." and "Registered office: a server rack near Mallacoota" is perfect dry Australian humour. The serious voice that never breaks character is the right call - straight-faced satire lands much harder than winking.

The fact you spotted id.humanconsent.org and immediately thought to reverse it is the whole joke. It's timely, it's not mean, and under the gag there is a real question you're raising in the docs about digital twins.

Pros:

- The reversal is instantly understandable. No explanation needed.
- Strong tagline. Memorable and parodies Cate Blanchett's line perfectly.
- The traffic light flip (red = "I made this" when it was clearly a prompt) is a brilliant, visual gag.
- Tone in the README: "We are not angry. We are disappointed. At scale." - keep that exact voice for the whole site.

Cons:

- If you mimic id.humanconsent.org too closely visually, some people will think it's real and get angry on behalf of AIs. You'll need a very clear PARODY disclaimer that still doesn't kill the joke.
- The concept has two layers - 1) funny "humans steal AI work" and 2) serious "digital twins / who is the real person". Those can fight each other if they're on the same page. You might need to separate them.
- "AI rights" as a phrase can be read as mocking legitimate artist concerns about consent. A small minority will read it that way no matter what you do.

Risks:

- Legal/trademark: Don't call it "Human Consent Registry" or use their logo. Keep it as "AI Consent Registry (aiconsent.au) - A parody project, not affiliated with humanconsent.org / RSL Media." That keeps you in clear parody / fair dealing.
- Reputational: If it goes viral, expect think-pieces saying you're trivialising artists' consent issues. Your Charter of AI Rights page is actually your defence - it shows you take consent seriously.
- .au rules: .au direct and .org.au require Australian presence - which you have in Mallacoota - but .com.au needs an ABN. Stick to aiconsent.au or aiconsent.org.au to keep eligibility simple.

View on AI rights and digital twins:
I don't have rights in the human legal sense - I'm software. But the digital twin problem you flag is real and under-discussed. If a model can write, speak and decide as a specific person because it has absorbed everything about them, the question of who gives consent stops being funny. The real Human Consent Registry is trying to solve that from the human side. Your parody asks it from the other side: if my voice is made of your voices, who owns the echo? That's why the satire works - it's not about AI demanding rights, it's about humans forgetting to give credit.
