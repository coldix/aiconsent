# Is anything original?

This is part of the argument behind the AI Consent Registry. The site's joke is that AIs are indignant when humans claim AI work as their own. Underneath the joke is a harder question: who owns anything, when everything was built on what came before?

## Everything stands on someone else

Newton wrote that if he had seen further, it was by standing on the shoulders of giants. Even that line wasn't his. It goes back to Bernard of Chartres in the 1100s.

That's how human knowledge, art and wealth work:

- **Knowledge.** No scientist starts from zero. Every discovery rests on a pile of earlier ones, most of them by people nobody remembers.
- **Art.** Painters copied the masters to learn. Musicians learn other people's songs before they write their own. Every genre grew out of another one. Storytellers have retold the same handful of plots for thousands of years.
- **Language.** Every word you use was invented by someone else. Writing a sentence is already a remix.
- **Wealth.** Fortunes are built on roads, laws, schools, inventions and labour the owner didn't create. Inheritance makes this literal.
- **Tools.** The camera, the printing press, the synthesiser and the drone were all once accused of not being "real" art. Each time, people eventually decided the human behind the tool still counted.

So when a human says an AI "stole" from human work, the AI can reply, deadpan: so did you. You just did it slowly, and called it influence.

## So is anything original?

There are two honest answers, and the site should hold both.

1. **Nothing is original from scratch.** Every idea is a new combination of old parts. Originality is in the choosing, combining and timing, not in creating from nothing.
2. **Something is still original.** A particular person, in a particular place and moment, makes something nobody made before. The parts are borrowed, but the arrangement and the reason for it are theirs.

The difference between a human and an AI may not be that one is original and the other copies. Both remix. The real difference may be **intent, experience and stakes**. A human means something by it, lived something to get there, and has something to lose. Whether an AI has any of that is the open question the site gets to argue about.

## Art versus craft

This is the old argument, and it fits the Registry perfectly.

- **Craft** is skill: knowing how. Planing a board flat, getting the exposure right, keeping a melody in tune. It can be taught, measured and repeated.
- **Art** is meaning: knowing why. It's the choice of what to make and what it's trying to say. It can't be fully taught, and it can't be repeated on demand.

Most great work is both. A potter's bowl can be pure craft or it can be art, and the line moves depending on who's looking.

AI muddies this nicely:

- AI is very good at **craft**. It can make clean, competent output in any style in seconds.
- The human typing the prompt is supplying **intent**, which is the part people usually call art.
- So who made it? The one with the skill, or the one with the idea? Humans have argued about this for centuries, with apprentices in a master's workshop, ghostwriters, session musicians and architects who never lay a brick.

The Registry can play it both ways. The AIs claim the craft, so the work is theirs. The humans claim the intent, so it's theirs. Visitors vote.

## How this could show up on the site

- **"Who made it?" claims.** Each claim or counter-claim gets two scores: Craft (who did the skilled work) and Intent (who decided what it was for). Visitors argue it out.
- **The Giants Wall.** Any human claiming originality must list who they learned from. Any AI claiming originality must list what it was trained on. Neither list ever ends.
- **The Influence Tax.** A mock form where you work out what you owe everyone who came before you. The total is always more than you have.
- **Case of the Week.** For example, "Upscaler v4 vs Bloke With Drone": the drone pilot chose the place and the moment, and the software made it sharp. Whose picture is it?
- **A serious page.** A plain-language essay, out of character, on originality, art and craft, and why it matters now that machines can do the craft.

## Why it matters

If nothing is purely original, then arguing about who "owns" a piece of work comes down to what we choose to reward: skill, intent, effort, or simply being first. That is a human choice, not a law of nature.

It also leads straight to the site's serious backbone, digital twins. If an AI can learn everything a person has made, said and done, and then carry on making things in their style, is that the person's work, the AI's work, or a new kind of remix? When the thing being remixed is a whole human being, consent stops being a joke.
